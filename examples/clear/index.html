One Clear button, two places

The Clear button below has one click handler and no idea what it clears. Beside the search field, the form empties the field. In the todo list, the list removes the completed tasks. Under the button is what pressing it would do right now, in the words of the section that answered, or why that section refuses. Drag the button from one place to the other, or use the two place buttons above them.

What to try

  1. Read the caption, then press Clear. The field empties, and the caption now says the field is already empty.
  2. Type in the field. The caption names what you typed.
  3. Drag Clear over the todo list's slot. Before you drop it, the slot says what Clear would do there. Drop it, and the caption under the button says the same. Untick a task and the count follows.
  4. Press Clear. The completed tasks go, and the caption says why the button is refused now. Add a task and tick it, and Clear has work again.
  5. Drag Clear back beside the search. The same button gets the form's answer again.

Who owns what

  • The button raises the intent ui:clear from itself when pressed, and that is all it does. It does not know what clearing means anywhere.
  • Each section is a scope with one interpreter for ui:clear, which reads the section and returns a plan. The search form reads its field, and the todo list reads its checkboxes. Neither knows about the button or about the other section.
  • The page owns the two slots, the drag and drop between them, and the caption. The caption calls interpret, which asks the same interpreter a press asks and runs nothing. A slot being dragged over calls it too, from the slot itself, since the slot sits under the same section the button would.

Limits

The caption is refreshed after any input, click or drop inside the demo. A change made from outside, by the console say, is not noticed until the next one. A refused button is dimmed with aria-disabled and still raises when pressed; the refusal is a plan with nothing to run, so nothing happens.

The drag is the browser's own drag and drop, so it needs a pointer. The two place buttons make the same move from the keyboard. Domocracy has no drag and drop of its own; the drop handler calls the region's move, as the place buttons do.
