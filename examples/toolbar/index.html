One toolbar, four rooms

The toolbar below has six buttons and not one handler that knows what they do. A button raises an intent, and the room the toolbar is in answers it: Apply commits a form, confirms a dialog, or ticks a card. Move the toolbar and the same buttons mean something else. Nothing in the toolbar changes, and the button you were on keeps its focus.

Who owns what

  • The toolbar owns being a toolbar: one tab stop, arrow keys between the buttons. Each button carries the intent it raises in data-intent and nothing else.
  • Each room owns a scope: one interpreter per intent type, which reads the room and returns a plan. The rooms know nothing about the toolbar or about each other. A card on the board is a room inside a room.
  • The page owns the dock, the travel between rooms, the status line and the two panels, and it runs the effects. The rehearsal panel asks the same interpreters intent would, under the same guard, and runs none of it.

What to try

  1. Hover or focus any button before pressing it. The panel says which rooms are on its route, what each would answer, and what the plan would do. Nothing has run.
  2. Press Apply while the toolbar is in the dock: nobody answers. Move it to the Form, change a field, and press Apply again.
  3. Focus Next room and press Enter repeatedly. The toolbar hops through the rooms and your focus stays on the button.
  4. In the Board, Apply reaches two scopes: the card ticks itself with continue and the board tallies with consume. Up and Down move the card with the toolbar inside it.
  5. In the Board, press Remove. The card's own scope hands the toolbar back to the dock first, then the board removes the card, as one plan in that order. Focus stays on Remove.
  6. Press the × on any card. It is a control like any other, and the same two scopes answer it.

Limits

A refusal here is a plan too: a consume with a status effect and no operations. That is this page's convention, not a library rule.

Focus survives a move where the browser has moveBefore. On the fallback path a moved button loses its focus, and the third and fifth items above will not hold.

The rehearsal catches a write that goes through a region, which is what the guard covers. An interpreter that calls node.remove() is a bug it cannot see, and the same is true when intent runs.

The dialog is a confirmation panel, not a modal. Nothing traps focus.
