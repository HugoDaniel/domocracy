One toolbar, four rooms

The two toolbars below have six buttons each and not one handler that knows what they do. A button raises an intent, and the room the toolbar is in answers it: Apply commits a form, confirms a dialog, or ticks a card. Move a toolbar and the same buttons mean something else. Nothing in the toolbar changes, and the button you were on keeps its focus. Under each button is what it would do here, in the room's own words, or why the room refuses.

A toolbar is one way to raise an intent and not the only one. A card's × raises Remove. The shortcuts below raise from whatever has focus: Alt+A in a form field is the form's Apply, and Alt+A on a card's checkbox is the card's. The rooms do not know who asked, and the same words come back either way.

Who owns what

  • A toolbar owns being a toolbar: one tab stop, arrow keys between the buttons, and a caption under each. Each button carries the intent it raises in data-intent and nothing else. The two toolbars know nothing of each other.
  • Each room owns a scope: one interpreter per intent type, which reads the room and returns a plan. The rooms know nothing about the toolbar or about each other. A card on the board is a room inside a room.
  • The page owns the dock, the travel between rooms, the shortcuts, the status line and the two panels, and it runs the effects. A travel request names its toolbar, or comes from inside one. The rehearsal panel calls interpret, which is the half of intent that writes nothing, and shows the answer in place: the route outlined on the rooms, and the nodes the plan names marked with what would happen to them.

Rooms answer nearest first and each adds to one plan. A room that answers continue adds its part and lets the next room add more. A room that answers consume adds its part and stops the collection, and what the nearer rooms added stays in the plan. A room with no interpreter for the intent is not asked, and a room after the one that consumed is never reached. The panel names each of those four for every room on the route.

Two words on a plan are this page's convention and not the library's. A plan that does something carries meaning, what it does in words. A plan that refuses carries refused, why. The interpreter decides once and explains alongside the operations; the trace keeps the plan as returned; and a control reads its caption and its availability from there. There is no second set of rules for what is enabled. A refused button is dimmed and still raises when pressed, and what runs is the refusal. A refusal is a consume, and a consume cannot take back what a nearer room already added: when a room refuses after another contributed, the caption says both, and the button stays available because pressing it still does the nearer room's part.

What to try

  1. Hover or focus any button before pressing it. The rooms on its route are outlined where they stand, the nodes the plan would touch are marked, and the panel says what each room answered. Nothing has run.
  2. Press Apply while the toolbar is in the dock: nobody answers. Move it to the Form and read the captions: Apply says the form is as committed. Change a field and the caption says how many fields Apply would commit.
  3. Focus Next room and press Enter repeatedly. The toolbar hops through the rooms and your focus stays on the button.
  4. In the Board, Apply reaches two scopes: the card ticks itself with continue and the board tallies with consume. Up and Down move the card with the toolbar inside it.
  5. In the Board, press Remove. The card's own scope hands the toolbar back to the dock first, then the board removes the card, as one plan in that order. Focus stays on Remove.
  6. Press Toolbar here on another card, then hover Up again. The same button now marks a different card, because the request came from a different place.
  7. Lock the card the toolbar is in and hover Remove. The lock is a scope on the slot, nearer than the card, and it consumes: the card and the board are never reached. Unlock, hover again, and the card continues, the board consumes, and the card's move stays in the plan.
  8. Press the × on any card. It is a control like any other, and the same two scopes answer it. The lock does not, because the × is beside the slot and not inside it.
  9. Put toolbar B in the Listbox and toolbar A in the Form. Read the two sets of captions. Then put both in the same card and press Apply on one: the other's caption changes too, because both ask the same card.
  10. Click into the Name field and press Alt+A, then select an option and press Alt+R, then focus a card's Lock and press Alt+↓. Each is the same intent a toolbar button raises, from where the focus is, and the panel lists what every shortcut would do from there.

Limits

A refusal here is a plan too: a consume with a status effect, a refused line and no operations. meaning and refused are this page's conventions; the library reads neither and keeps the plan they are on.

The captions are as current as the last change the page noticed, which is any input or click inside the demo and any intent it raised. A change made to the tree from outside, by the console say, is not noticed until the next one.

Focus survives a move where the browser has moveBefore. On the fallback path a moved button loses its focus, and the third and fifth items above will not hold.

The rehearsal catches a write that goes through a region, which is what the guard covers. An interpreter that calls node.remove() is a bug it cannot see, and the same is true when intent runs.

The highlight is two attributes the page writes on the scopes of the route and on the nodes a plan names, and clears before the next rehearsal. It is presentation: no interpreter reads them, and a region owns the order and the lifetime of its children and not their attributes.

The shortcuts use Alt, which is Option on a Mac, with the physical key, so a letter is read before the system turns it into another character. A browser or a system that claims one of the combinations keeps it, and the page does not see the press. Alt+N from a control that is in no toolbar is refused, because Next room moves the toolbar the request came from.

The dialog is a confirmation panel, not a modal. Nothing traps focus.
